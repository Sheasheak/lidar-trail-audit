LiDAR Trail Audit Tool

Automated mountain-bike trail grading from LiDAR elevation data, assessed against the NZ MTB Trail Design & Construction Guidelines (Recreation Aotearoa, 2022). Give it a trail line and it works out how hard the trail actually is — no stopwatch, no guesswork, no arguing about whether it's "really" a Blue or a Black.

Test trail: Askins, Christchurch Port Hills — downhill only, Blue grade, 2116 m long, 192 m descent.

What it does

You give it a trail line (a KML export from Trailforks, a GPS track, or one drawn in the bundled trail editor). It samples 1 m bare-earth LiDAR elevation data along that line and computes, at every station:

  • Gradient — smoothed over a moving window, with an "exceptions" allowance for short steep pinches
  • Turn radius — fitted through each point on the line to catch tight switchbacks
  • Cross-slope — perpendicular transects off the DEM, how steeply the hill falls away across the tread
  • Exposure — 90th-percentile fall height alongside the trail
  • Annual rainfall — 5-year average, as a proxy for surface durability

Each of those gets graded against the Recreation Aotearoa thresholds below, and the trail's overall grade is the worst of them. A short field questionnaire (tread obstacles, trail surface, technical features) fills in the parts LiDAR can't see, and the result is shown as an interactive map and a 3D terrain view with the trail line draped over it.

How it works — the pipeline

  1. Trail line (KML) → list of (lon, lat) points in WGS84
  2. Reproject WGS84 → NZTM2000 (EPSG:2193) so distances are in metres
  3. Interpolate to a fixed spacing along the line
  4. Sample elevation from local LINZ 1 m LiDAR DEM tiles
  5. Compute gradient between consecutive points, smoothed over a window
  6. Compute turn radius via the circumradius of point triples
  7. Compute cross-slope from perpendicular transects, and exposure/rainfall from surrounding terrain
  8. Grade every measurement against the Recreation Aotearoa thresholds
  9. Render an interactive map, gradient profile, and 3D terrain view

Using it

1. Set up

On first run it asks for a folder of local .asc LiDAR DEM tiles (downloaded from LINZ Data Service, covering the area of the trail). Point it at that folder once and it's remembered.

2. Grade a trail

Use the bundled Askins test line, or upload your own KML. Choose whether the trail is descending or climbing/two-way (this changes which gradient ceiling applies), and optionally tune the sampling settings:

  • Station spacing — how densely the line is walked
  • Gradient window — how far the moving average runs
  • Turn-radius chord — the arc length fitted through each point
  • Cross-slope offset — how far either side of the tread the transect samples
  • Terrain margin / grid resolution — controls the 3D surface drawn under the trail

3. Read the result

An interactive map colours the trail by gradient grade and marks tight corners, with a stats panel showing every measurement and its grade side by side. A field questionnaire (obstacles, surface, technical features) combines with the LiDAR-derived grade to give the final overall grade. There's also a full 3D terrain view with the trail line draped over the DEM.
