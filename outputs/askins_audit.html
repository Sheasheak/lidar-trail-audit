Inputs

What the audit was run on

Sources

Two data sources: a GPS trace of where the trail is, and a LiDAR surface of what the ground does there.

Trail line data/askins.kml 112 GPS vertices exported from Trailforks, WGS84
Elevation LINZ LiDAR 1 m DEM — layer 121859 Bare-earth national amalgamation; Canterbury tile flown 2024–25
Access LINZ Raster Query API, threaded Point queries at each station, cached by line hash
Local raster data/askins_dem.tif 960 × 720 merged DEM, EPSG:2193 — used for the editor overlays
Standard NZ MTB Trail Design & Construction Guidelines Recreation Aotearoa, 2022 — descending trail thresholds

Parameters

The settings that turn a line and a surface into a grade. Change any one of these and the result moves.

Working CRSNZTM2000 (EPSG:2193) Metric — all distances, gradients and radii computed here
Resample spacing1 m along the line Reported at every 5th station below
Gradient window50 m moving average First and last 25 m excluded from statistics — the window is only part-filled there
Turn radiusCircumradius, 5 m chord Straights return infinity and are excluded from the radius test
Radius cutoff500 m Anything above this counts as straight
Grading ruleWorst of gradient and radius Both a strict and an exception-aware pass are reported

Pipeline

Each step's output is the next step's input.

  1. Read the KML into lon/lat pairspykml → 112 vertices
  2. Reproject WGS84 → NZTM2000pyproj, always_xy — metres from here on
  3. Resample the line to even spacingshapely interpolate → 2 118 stations
  4. Query ground elevation per stationLINZ raster API, 10 threads; nulls dropped
  5. Differentiate and smoothrise over run, 50 m moving average
  6. Fit turn radius through point triplescircumradius; collinear → straight
  7. Test each station against the thresholdsgradient grade and radius grade, worst wins
  8. Roll up to a trail gradestrict worst-case, then with exception allowances

Grading thresholds — descending trails

Recreation Aotearoa, 2022. A grade allows a share of its length to break the maximum — that allowance is the exception column.

Why LiDAR — GPS elevation vs bare-earth ground

The same line, elevation from two sources. The handheld GPS trace sits systematically high and noisy; the LiDAR surface is the ground. Grading off the GPS heights called 31% of the trail steep — the LiDAR says 16%.
